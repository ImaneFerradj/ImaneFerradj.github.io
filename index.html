PhD candidate in robotics, Arts et Métiers, Paris

Soft, steerable robots
for bladder inspection.

I'm Imane Ferradj. I work with electroactive polymers, thin films that bend when you apply a voltage. My PhD is about understanding these actuators, controlling them, and using them to steer a miniature continuum robot for telecystoscopy.

Detailed profile Publications

The idea: a passive shaft goes in through the urethra, and a short steerable tip does the looking around inside the bladder.

The research

From the material to the robot.

I started with the material itself, measuring how it responds to an electric field. From there I modeled the actuators, learned to control how they bend, and used all of it to design the tip of a small robot for bladder inspection.

1 · The material

How the polymer behaves.

I measured how these polymers respond to an electric field, and how their composition changes that response. This gave me the parameters for every model that came after.

2 · The actuator

One actuator, three sections.

I designed a printed actuator split into three sections that can be driven separately, so the whole structure can be shaped instead of bent in a single arc.

3 · Control

Making it bend where I want.

These materials have hysteresis: the same voltage doesn't always give the same bend. I work on compensating for it, and on control methods that scale to robots with many sections.

4 · The robot

Designing the robot's tip.

All of this feeds into the design of a small steerable tip for bladder inspection, sized and laid out to reach and see as much of the bladder as possible.

The design

What it looks like.

The actuator is printed in three sections that can be driven separately. The robot's tip is a small tube carrying a camera, LEDs and air channels, with the actuators laid along it either straight or wrapped in a helix.
